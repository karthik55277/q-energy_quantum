How it works (for the report)

1. Plans as qubits. Each appliance gets 2 qubits for its time: 00 Morning, 01 Noon, 10 Evening, 11 Night. Four appliances use 8 qubits, which together hold 2⁸ = 256 plans at once (superposition).

2. Oracle. The computer checks each plan against your limits and the allowed times, and the oracle flips the sign of the good ones (the red flash). This checking step is classical, so this is a hybrid quantum-classical design.

3. Diffuser. Every amplitude is reflected about the average. Flipped (good) plans jump up; the rest shrink a little.

4. Repeat k times, then measure. The best number of rounds is k ≈ π/4 × √(N/M), where N = 256 plans and M = good plans. Measuring gives one plan, with probability equal to amplitude².

Why it matters. Checking plans one by one needs on average N/M tries. Grover needs about √(N/M) rounds, a quadratic saving that grows with bigger homes. The app builds this circuit with Qiskit and runs it 1,000 times on the Aer quantum simulator.
